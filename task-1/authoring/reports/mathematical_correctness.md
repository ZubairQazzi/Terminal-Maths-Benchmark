# Constructive feasibility and proofs

For a monic q, companion C(q) satisfies q(C)=0. The identity `(zI-C) sum_{j=1}^d q_j sum_{k=0}^{j-1} z^{j-1-k} C^k = q(z)I` gives the finite-block inverse. Infinite `(I-zJ)^-1=sum z^j J^j` is polynomial. Right `[-zI | shifted I]` has a polynomial right inverse obtained by fixing its first free coordinate; transpose yields a left inverse. These block inverses are reflexive and both projectors polynomial. If U P V=K, then G=V G_K U has the required properties, with D the largest nonzero Smith factor. Canonical singular generators `(1,z,...,z^e)` transformed by V or U^T have constant selector duals transformed by V^-1 or U^-T, proving saturation. This establishes existence over Q for every promised input, including zero rank and empty dimensions.

Let G=N/D be any valid submitted generalized inverse, with Q=GP polynomial. If Px=f and tx is polynomial, then `tGf=Q(tx)` is polynomial. Thus the least common denominator of Gf divides every solution denominator. Conversely Gf is a solution for compatible f. Consequently cancelling h=gcd(D, components of Nf) gives the intrinsic least monic denominator delta=D/h, independent of the chosen complement. Kernel additions cannot reduce a pole.

For p^a exactly dividing delta, E=nu_p(D). Choose a row of N whose response has valuation E-a. Since NP is divisible by D, the row annihilates P modulo p^E. Reducing its entries modulo p^E preserves both the annihilator property and the response valuation. Any solution with common denominator t obeys wP(tx)=t(wf), so nu_p(t)>=a. The identity Py=delta f gives the matching upper bound. The verifier checks these local witnesses independently and does not rerun the oracle's gcd algorithm.

The frame algorithm first reduces one full-normal-rank specialization to diag(Ir,0). Matrix-pair homomorphism equations then constrain X and Y to share their top-left block, with forced zero off-diagonal blocks. Their remaining coefficients satisfy a rational linear system. Constant strict equivalence guarantees that its solution space contains an invertible pair. A reproducibly seeded 512-attempt fast phase is followed by a finite complete grid search. The product of the shared top-left and two free diagonal-block determinants is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most m+n; a grid with m+n+1 values per coordinate cannot vanish everywhere. Thus a valid strict equivalence is eventually found exactly. This fallback can be exponentially expensive: no worst-case polynomial runtime claim is made. All validated inputs succeed in the fast phase, and both original pair identities are checked before a frame is returned.
